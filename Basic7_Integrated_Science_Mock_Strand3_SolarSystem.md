### Question 6 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- The orbit of a planet is the path it follows as it moves around the Sun.
- It is the curved path the planet travels along, again and again.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). Accept 'the path a planet takes round the Sun'.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- An elliptical path is an oval path - a flattened circle, not a perfect circle.
- It has two 'centres' (foci) instead of one.
- The Sun is not at the centre of the ellipse, but off to one side.
- A planet's orbit is an ellipse.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). Accept 'an oval' for an ellipse. Award at least 1 mark for 'it is an oval, not a perfect circle'.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- Because the path is an ellipse and the Sun is off to one side of it, the planet is nearer the Sun at one part of the path and further away at another.
- So the distance between the planet and the Sun changes as the planet moves along its orbit.
- The planet is closest to the Sun at one end of the ellipse and furthest at the other.
- The Sun lies at one of the two foci of the ellipse, not at its centre.
- If the orbit were a perfect circle with the Sun at the centre, the distance would never change.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct points (4 marks). The key ideas are: the Sun is off-centre, so the distance changes, and the Sun lies at a focus rather than at the centre. Award at least 2 marks for 'the Sun is not at the centre, so the distance changes'.*

- Arrange the planets in the correct order from the Sun: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune.
- Hang them from a wooden frame or a cardboard base with string, or fix them on sticks.
- Show the path of each planet with string, wire or a drawn ellipse, not a perfect circle.
- Add the Moon beside the Earth, and add rings to Saturn.
- Write a title and a key, and let each group member take a task.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct points (4 marks). This is a design task: award method marks for any sensible step. The three things to look for first are: the correct ORDER of the planets, the Sun at the centre, and materials a real class could find.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- A model shows the order of the planets and their relative sizes in a way a drawing cannot.
- The class can handle it, walk round it and point, so the idea stays in the memory.
- Building it together makes pupils discuss and explain the solar system to one another.
- It shows the distances and the shapes of the paths (the orbits), which a flat drawing often gets wrong.
- One thing it must show: the planets in the correct order from the Sun (or: the paths as ellipses, not perfect circles; or: the Sun at the centre).
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO sensible reasons and 1 mark for a correct thing the model must show (3 marks). Accept any reason that shows the model adds something a book cannot. Do NOT accept 'it is fun' on its own.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The two indicators name only Mercury and Venus specifically (B7/JHS1.3.2.1.2), so Questions 4 and 5 are built around those two planets. Earth and Mars are tested only as members of the inner four, which is what B7/JHS1.3.2.1.1 asks for.
- Question 3 is the 'identify the inner planets from a picture' exemplar. The diagram is drawn with text characters: redraw it with real circles or paste in a printed picture - the numbering (1-4) is what the questions depend on. Key: 1 Mercury, 2 Venus, 3 Earth, 4 Mars.
- Question 4(c) targets the most common misconception in this topic: that the planet nearest the Sun must be the hottest. The expected answer is the thick carbon-dioxide atmosphere of Venus that traps heat. Expect many pupils to give the wrong answer here.
- Question 7 is the 'design and construct a model' exemplar. Because it is open, award method marks generously: any sensible step earns credit. Look first for the correct order of the planets, the Sun at the centre, and materials a real class could find. If your class actually builds the model, accept what they did as evidence.
- Question 6(c) expects only that the Sun is off to one side of the ellipse and that the distance therefore changes. Do NOT expect the word 'focus' or any statement of Kepler's laws - that is beyond B7.
- Topic 8.1 carries 19 of 100 marks, the highest weighting, because defining the solar system and building the model are the opening and closing tasks of the indicator. If you want a flatter spread, move marks from Questions 1 and 7 to Questions 2 and 5.
- If you have printed pictures, a chart or a short video of the solar system, use them during revision: exemplar 1 of B7/JHS1.3.2.1.1 specifically asks pupils to identify the inner planets using pictures and videos.
