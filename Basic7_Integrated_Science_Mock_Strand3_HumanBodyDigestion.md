- To protect the body against disease and keep it healthy.

**(a)** [3 marks]

- The inner wall of the small intestine is covered with many tiny finger-like villi.
- The small soluble molecules of digested food pass through the thin wall of the villi.
- They pass into the blood in the tiny blood vessels inside the villi.
- The blood then carries the digested food to the liver and round the body to the parts that need it.
- Fatty acids and glycerol pass first into the lymph before reaching the blood.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The key ideas are: through the thin wall of the villi, into the blood, and carried round the body.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Its inner wall is folded and covered with thousands of tiny villi, which give it a very large surface area for absorption.
- Its wall is very thin - only one cell thick - so the digested food has only a short distance to travel.
- It is long, so the food takes a long time to pass through and there is time to absorb it.
- It has a rich supply of blood vessels, which carry the absorbed food away quickly and so keep absorption going.
- Each villus has a lymph vessel to carry away the products of fat digestion.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The single most important point is the large surface area given by the villi; award at least 1 mark for it.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- The part that cannot be digested - mostly roughage or fibre and dead cells - passes into the large intestine.
- In the large intestine most of the remaining water and mineral salts are absorbed.
- The material that is left becomes more solid and is formed into faeces.
- The faeces are stored in the rectum and then pass out of the body through the anus.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The answer must say WHERE it happens (the large intestine) to earn full marks; award at most 2 marks if the place is not named.*

### Question 7 (7 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- (i) a blue-black colour.
- (ii) Benedict's solution, added to the food and heated in a water bath (Fehling's solution may also be used).
- (iii) a violet (purple) colour.
- (iv) rub a small amount of the food on a piece of paper - the grease-spot test.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for all FOUR blanks correctly filled (4 marks). The blank number must be matched to the correct answer. For (ii) accept 'Benedict's solution and heat'. For (iv) accept 'the grease-spot test' or an ethanol emulsion test with a cloudy white result.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Crush or grind each sample and put a small amount of each into separate test tubes or dishes, and label them.
- Gari: add a few drops of iodine solution; a blue-black colour shows that starch is present.
- Beans: crush them, add Biuret solution (or sodium hydroxide and copper(II) sulphate solution); a violet colour shows that protein is present.
- Palm oil: rub a little on a piece of paper; a translucent spot shows that fat or oil is present.
- Keep one tube or dish untreated as a control, so the class can see what a negative result looks like.
- Write down what is seen for each sample in a table.
- Safety: do not taste any of the samples, handle iodine and Biuret solution with care, wear eye protection, heat only in a water bath, and wash hands afterwards.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). This is a design task, so award method marks: any sensible step earns credit. The three things to look for first are: a test for each of the three samples, the expected result for each, and a safety precaution. Do NOT award a mark for a plan that tells pupils to taste an unknown sample.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Question 3 is the 'identify and draw the digestive system' exemplar. The diagram is drawn with text characters: redraw it with real boxes or paste in a hand-drawn figure - the numbering (1-5) is what the questions depend on. Key: 1 mouth, 2 oesophagus, 3 stomach, 4 small intestine, 5 large intestine.
- Question 3(b) is a common trip-up. The stomach and intestines are part of the alimentary canal, so they are NOT accepted here; the expected answers are the liver, the pancreas and the salivary glands.
- Question 7 is the practical food-test exemplar. If you can, run it before the mock: iodine on gari or bread, Biuret on beans or egg white, and the grease-spot test with palm oil. Pupils who have seen the colours answer far better.
- Safety for any practical follow-up: no tasting of unknown samples, iodine and Biuret solution are irritants, Benedict's solution must be heated in a water bath and never over a naked flame, and hands must be washed afterwards. The marking note withholds credit from a plan that tells pupils to taste an unknown sample.
- The paper deliberately does NOT name enzymes. B7 expects 'saliva begins the digestion of starch' and 'gastric juice begins the digestion of protein' - do not reward answers that go beyond this, and do not penalise pupils for not using the words amylase or pepsin.
- Topic 7.7 carries 12 of 100 marks, the lightest weighting, only because it is the last topic and much of it is practical. If you have a well-equipped laboratory and want a more practical paper, move marks from Question 6 to Question 7.
- The digestive-system diagram and both tables are laid out for A4 portrait. Check the page break before Question 3 and before Question 7 when you print.
