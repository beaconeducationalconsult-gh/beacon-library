### Question 1 (12 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Energy cannot be created and it cannot be destroyed.
- Energy can only be changed from one form into another.
- The total amount of energy always stays the same.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The three ideas to look for are: cannot be created, cannot be destroyed, can be changed from one form to another. Award at most 2 marks if one of these is missing.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- A closed system is one in which no energy enters and no energy leaves.
- All the energy stays inside the system; none escapes into the surroundings.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). The key idea is that nothing crosses the boundary of the system.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- The dry cell stores chemical energy.
- The chemical energy is changed into electrical energy in the wires.
- The electrical energy is then changed into light energy and heat energy in the bulb.
- No energy is lost - the total amount at the end is exactly the same as the chemical energy that was in the dry cell at the start.
- Some of the energy has simply changed into forms that are not useful, such as heat.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct points (4 marks). The pupil must trace the chain chemical to electrical to light and heat AND say that the total stays the same; award at most 3 marks if the total is not mentioned.*

**(d)** [3 marks]

- The law tells us that the claim is impossible.
- Energy cannot be created, so a machine cannot give out more energy than it was given.
- The most the machine could do is change all the energy it was given into other forms.
- A machine that appears to do this is only releasing energy that was already stored in it.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for saying the claim is impossible and 1 mark each for any TWO correct reasons (3 marks). Accept 'it cannot be done' or 'the machine would not work'. Do NOT accept 'the machine would work at night'.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Switch off lights, fans and the television when nobody is in the room.
- Use energy-saving bulbs or LED bulbs instead of ordinary ones.
- Put a lid on the pot when cooking, so less heat escapes and less fuel is used.
- Do not boil more water in the kettle than is needed.
- Close the fridge door quickly and do not leave it open.
- Iron all the clothes at one time instead of heating the iron again and again.
- Dry clothes and food in the sun instead of using a dryer.
- Use a solar panel or a rechargeable battery where possible.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct ways (4 marks). Accept any sensible way of saving energy in the home or at school.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- The law says energy cannot be destroyed, but it does not say the energy stays in a form we can use.
- When fuel is burnt or electricity is used, the useful energy is changed into forms such as heat and sound that spread out and cannot easily be gathered up again.
- So the amount of energy that is easy to use - fuel, electricity - keeps going down, even though the total amount of energy stays the same.
- Conserving energy means slowing down the change of useful energy into forms we cannot use.
- It also saves money and makes the fuel we have last longer.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct points (4 marks). The key idea is the difference between the TOTAL energy (which stays the same) and the USEFUL energy (which does not). Award at most 3 marks if the pupil does not make that distinction clear.*

### Question 7 (7 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- It saves the family money, because less electricity and less fuel are bought.
- It makes the fuel and the electricity we have last longer, so they are there for the future.
- It means fewer power cuts and less pressure on the national grid.
- It reduces smoke and fumes in the home, so the family breathes cleaner air.
- The money saved can be used for other things the family needs, such as school books.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). Accept any sensible benefit to human beings.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Fewer trees are cut down for firewood and charcoal, so forest animals keep their habitat and birds keep their nesting places.
- Less smoke and fewer gases go into the air, so plants and animals are not damaged by pollution.
- Rivers and streams are not dammed or polluted as much, so fish and other water life survive.
- Less fuel is burnt, so the climate is not warmed as quickly and animals are not forced to move.
- Fewer forests are cleared, so the plants that give out oxygen are left standing for all living things.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct points (4 marks). At least TWO of the four must be clearly about OTHER LIFE FORMS (plants or animals), not about people; award at most 2 marks if all the points are about human beings.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- The whole paper turns on ONE distinction, and it is the one pupils get wrong most: the law of conservation of energy says the TOTAL energy stays the same; conserving energy for future use means looking after the USEFUL energy, because it keeps changing into forms we cannot gather up again. Question 6(b) tests exactly that, and the marking note allows at most 3 of 4 marks if the pupil does not make the distinction clear. Teach it explicitly before the mock.
- Fig. 1 is drawn with text characters because this is a plain Markdown file. Redraw it before printing if you can, but the numbering is what the questions depend on. Key: (i) chemical, (ii) electrical, (iii) light, (iv) heat.
- Question 1(d) asks pupils to judge a man who claims he has built a machine that gives out more energy than it is given. There are no marks for 'maybe' - the law says it is impossible, and the two marks are for the reasons. This is the item that separates pupils who have understood the law from those who have only memorised its wording.
- Question 5(a) is a design task: award method marks generously. Any workable diagram that is clearly described earns credit - the three marks are for the overall design, the labels, and showing at least two real conversions.
- Question 7(b) is the 'benefit to humans and other life forms' part of B7/JHS1.4.3.1.3. Insist on at least two points that are genuinely about plants or animals; a list of four human benefits earns only 2 of the 4 marks.
- The only arithmetic in the paper is Question 11 (100 J in, 20 J as light, so 80 J as heat). It applies the law rather than a formula, and it is the single Challenging item in Section A on this topic. Do not extend it into efficiency percentages - that is not B7 work.
- Topic weighting is 23 marks for 13.1 and 25 to 26 for each of the other three topics. If you would rather weight the everyday examples more heavily, move marks towards Questions 4 and 5.
- A good practical lesson before this mock: take a torch to pieces with the class and trace the chain dry cell, wires, bulb - chemical, electrical, light and heat. That is exemplar 1 of B7/JHS1.4.3.1.1, and it makes Questions 1(c) and 2 straightforward.
