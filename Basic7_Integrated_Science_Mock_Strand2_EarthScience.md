### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Rain
- Rivers
- Streams

  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct natural sources (4 marks). Accept 'wells' only if the pupil makes clear that they reach ground water. Do NOT accept 'tap water', 'a dam', 'a water tank' or 'sachet water'.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Surface water flows over the land (rivers, lakes, streams) while underground water is held in the soil and rocks below the surface.
- Surface water is easy to see and to reach while underground water has to be reached by digging a well or drilling a borehole.
- Surface water dries up quickly in a drought while underground water lasts longer.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct differences (2 marks). The difference must say something about BOTH types of water, not just one.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Rain water comes straight from the clouds.
- It has not flowed over or through the ground, so it has not dissolved minerals and salts from the soil and rocks.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for stating that rain falls directly from the clouds. 1 mark for stating that it has not picked up dissolved minerals / salts from the ground. Do NOT award a mark for 'it is safe to drink' - rain water can still collect dust and germs from the air.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

### Question 3 (6 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- Evaporation is the loss of water from surfaces such as rivers, lakes, oceans and wet soil, while transpiration is the loss of water vapour from the leaves of plants.
- Evaporation happens from any wet surface; transpiration happens only in plants.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). The answer must make the difference clear. Do NOT award a mark for naming the two processes without a difference.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Water vapour rises into the air from evaporation and transpiration.
- As it rises it cools.
- The cooled vapour condenses into tiny droplets of water around small dust particles.
- Millions of these tiny droplets gather together to form a cloud.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). Accept a clear two-step answer (vapour rises and cools, then condenses into droplets).*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- It soaks into the ground to become ground water (it improves the water table).
- It runs over the surface into streams, rivers, lakes and the sea.
- It is taken up by the roots of plants.
- It evaporates back into the air.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). Accept 'it is stored in a dam' as an additional correct point.*

### Question 4 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- The same stages - evaporation, condensation and precipitation - follow each other in the same order over and over again.
- Water keeps moving round and round between the earth and the air; it never stops or starts at one point.
- The pattern can be seen happening again and again in many different places.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). Accept any answer that gives the idea of the same sequence happening repeatedly. Do NOT award three marks for the same point repeated.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- The sun keeps supplying the energy needed for evaporation.
- Water keeps moving between the surface of the earth and the air.
- Gravity keeps pulling the water back down as precipitation.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct reasons (2 marks). Accept 'the sun never stops shining on the earth' with the link to evaporation.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Water is not used up - it only changes form and moves from one place to another.
- The water we use goes back into the environment (through washing, sweat, sewage and evaporation).
- The water cycle collects it, cleans it by evaporation and returns it as rain.
- So the same water is recycled over and over again.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The key idea is recycling rather than new water being made. Do NOT award a mark for 'because new water is created'.*

**(d)** [2 marks]

- A cycle returns to where it started, and the water cycle does exactly that: water leaves the surface, forms clouds and falls back to the surface.
- A straight line has a beginning and an end, but the water cycle has neither - it keeps going round and round.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). Accept a clearly explained 'it comes back to the start'.*

### Question 5 (10 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- It provides clean water for drinking, washing and farming.
- It carries nutrients to plants and animals.
- It improves the water table (replaces underground water).
- It regulates the weather pattern of a place.
- It releases energy that warms the environment.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). All five points above are taken from the curriculum exemplar and are equally acceptable.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- The water table is the level below which the ground is full of water.
- Rain soaks into the soil and rocks and refills the underground water store.
- So the water cycle keeps the water table from falling and keeps wells and boreholes supplied with water.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). Award at least 1 mark for the idea that rainwater soaks into the ground and replaces underground water.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- Rain water dissolves minerals in the soil as it soaks in.
- The water carries these dissolved minerals (nutrients) down to the roots of plants.
- Plants take up the nutrients with the water and use them to grow.
- Extra water also washes nutrients from decaying matter into the soil where roots can reach them.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct points (4 marks). Award at least 1 mark for stating that water carries dissolved minerals to the roots of plants. Accept 'rivers carry fertile silt on to farm land' as one correct point.*

### Question 6 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- It determines how much rain falls in a place and when.
- It determines how cloudy or sunny the weather is.
- It affects how humid (how much water vapour is in the air) a place is.
- It affects temperature, because evaporation and condensation move heat around.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). Accept 'it brings the rainy season and the dry season'.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Trees lose a lot of water vapour through transpiration.
- This adds extra water vapour to the air above the forest.
- More water vapour means more condensation, so more clouds form.
- With more clouds there is a greater chance of rain.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The chain must link transpiration, extra water vapour and increased cloud/rain formation. Do NOT award a mark for 'trees attract rain' on its own.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- In the harmattan the wind blows from the Sahara Desert, across the dry lands to the north.
- That air contains very little water vapour.
- With little water vapour there is little condensation, so few clouds form and no rain falls.
- The same wind carries fine dust from the desert, which makes the air dusty and hazy.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct points (4 marks). Award at least 1 mark for naming the wind from the Sahara and 1 mark for the link to very little water vapour.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Observation: droplets of water form on the outside of the cold glass.
- Conclusion: water vapour in the warm air touched the cold glass, cooled down and changed back into liquid water.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for the observation (droplets on the outside of the glass). 1 mark for the conclusion that water vapour in the air cooled and turned back to liquid. Do NOT accept 'the water leaked through the glass'.*

- Condensation.
