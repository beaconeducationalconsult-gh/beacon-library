### Question 1 (9 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Any four: the sun or solar energy; the wind; moving water or water behind a dam; biogas from animal dung and food waste; crops and crop waste grown again each season; the heat inside the earth.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct sources (4 marks). Do NOT accept firewood, charcoal, petrol, diesel, kerosene, coal or LPG - none of these will last. Do NOT accept 'electricity' on its own, because it depends on the source it is made from.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- A renewable source is one that is replaced naturally as fast as people use it, so it never runs out.
- It is also a source that can go on being used without spoiling the air, the land or the climate for people in the future.
  - *Marking note: Up to 3 marks. 1 mark for 'it is replaced naturally'; 1 mark for 'as fast as it is used, so it does not run out'; and 1 mark for 'without harming the environment'. An answer that only says 'it is good energy' earns 0.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Any two: fewer trees are cut down, so the woodland around the village is saved; the goat waste is used instead of being left to rot; the gas burns without smoke, so the kitchen and the family's lungs stay cleaner; the slurry left over is a good manure for the farm; the family spends less on charcoal or firewood; the gas is free once the digester is built.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct reasons (2 marks). Credit 'the trees are saved', 'the waste is used', 'no smoke', 'free manure' and 'it costs less'. Do NOT credit a reason that just repeats 'it is renewable'.*

### Question 2 (8 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- Any two: it is made from trees, and trees take many years to grow back; making it destroys the forest around the village; the wood is being used faster than it can be replaced; burning it gives out smoke and carbon dioxide.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct reasons (2 marks). The reason must be about the SOURCE running out or being used up, not merely that it is dirty or expensive.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct alternatives (3 marks). Credit the improved cookstove only if the pupil is clear that it reduces the wood used rather than replacing the source. Do NOT accept firewood or charcoal, which is what they are moving away from.*

- Accept any one real difficulty, explained. The digester or the cooker costs money to buy or build, and the family may not have it. LPG cylinders must be refilled, and the dealer may be far away. Nobody in the family may know how to look after the new equipment.
  - *Marking note: Up to 3 marks. 1 mark for naming a real difficulty; 1 mark for explaining why it is a problem for THIS family; and 1 mark for a difficulty that shows real thought, such as the season, the distance to a supplier, or the daily work of feeding a digester. Cap at 1 if the difficulty is vague or would apply to anything at all.*

### Question 3 (9 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- Any two: carbon dioxide, methane, nitrous oxide, water vapour.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct gases (2 marks). Do NOT accept oxygen or nitrogen, which make up most of the air but do not trap heat in this way.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Heat from the sun reaches the earth and warms the ground. The warm ground gives the heat back out again, as infrared radiation.
- Greenhouse gases in the air let the sun's heat pass down but they absorb much of the heat going back up, and they send some of it back towards the ground instead of letting it escape into space.
- So the heat is held near the surface, and the air near the ground stays warmer than it would otherwise be.
  - *Marking note: Up to 4 marks. 1 mark for heat from the sun reaching the earth; 1 mark for the earth giving heat back out; 1 mark for the gases absorbing that heat; and 1 mark for the heat being sent back down or trapped near the surface. An answer that only says 'they trap heat' earns 1.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- HELPFUL: without any greenhouse gases the earth would be far too cold for living things, so the natural greenhouse effect keeps the planet warm enough for us to live on.
- HARMFUL: people are now adding far more of these gases - by burning fuel, cutting down the forest and keeping cattle - so too much heat is being trapped and the earth is warming too fast.
- That extra warming brings floods at the coast, unreliable rain, crop failure and hunger, so it is the EXTRA greenhouse effect that harms us, not the effect itself.
  - *Marking note: Up to 3 marks. 1 mark for the helpful part - the earth would be too cold without it; 1 mark for the harmful part - extra gas traps too much heat; and 1 mark for a consequence such as flooding, unreliable rain or crop failure. A pupil who says only that the greenhouse effect is bad earns at most 1.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Any three: fit solar panels on the roof; use a solar water heater; cook with an improved cookstove or LPG instead of firewood; install a biogas digester for the dining-hall waste; use LED bulbs instead of filament bulbs; use a wind pump to draw water; switch off lights, fans and computers when they are not needed; insulate the roof; plant trees round the compound for shade.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct choices (3 marks). Do NOT accept 'use electricity' on its own - the point is where the energy comes from. Do NOT accept the same choice written twice in different words.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Accept any two correctly explained. Solar panels: materials in the panel take in light energy from the sun and change it straight into electrical energy, with nothing burnt and no smoke. Improved cookstove: a wall round the fire keeps the heat in and sends it up to the pot, and the controlled air lets the wood burn more completely, so less wood boils the same pot. LED bulb: it gives out light without first getting hot, so far less of the electricity is wasted as heat. Biogas digester: bacteria rot the waste without air and give off methane, which burns as a fuel.
  - *Marking note: Up to 4 marks: 2 marks for each of the two explanations. For each, 1 mark for the science and 1 mark for saying why it saves energy or helps the environment. A description with no scientific idea earns only 1.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Accept any sensible check, for example: read the electricity meter before and after and compare the bills; count how many bags of firewood the kitchen buys each term and compare with last term; keep a daily record of how long the lights are on; check that the LED bulbs are still working after a term.
  - *Marking note: Up to 2 marks. 1 mark for a check that MEASURES something real, and 1 mark for comparing it with what happened before - the comparison is what shows a saving. A pupil who says only 'we will see if it works' earns 0.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- A solar dryer is a closed box or cabinet, often with a clear cover, that lets the sun's heat in and keeps it there, so the air inside gets hotter than the air outside and the water in the fish evaporates faster.
- Because the fish is inside a covered box, it is protected from dust, flies and sudden rain, so much less of it is spoiled.
- The fish dries evenly and more quickly, keeps its quality, and so fetches a better price at the market.
  - *Marking note: Up to 4 marks. 1 mark for the sun's heat being trapped or the air inside getting hotter; 1 mark for the water evaporating from the fish; 1 mark for protection from dust, flies and rain; and 1 mark for the result - better fish and a better price. Cap at 2 if the pupil only says 'it dries the fish faster'.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- The whole community benefits, not only she does. Less fish is spoiled, so more food reaches the market and families nearby have fish to buy; good dried fish travels further, so it can be sold in other towns and brings money into the town; because the fish keeps, it can be eaten in the lean season when the catch is poor; and less spoiled fish is thrown away, so there is less waste and fewer flies breeding near the market.
- A strong answer may add that she herself gains the most directly, since her income rises, but that the benefit spreads well beyond her.
  - *Marking note: Up to 4 marks. 1 mark for taking a clear position; 1 mark for a benefit to people other than the fishmonger; 1 mark for a second such benefit, such as food in the lean season or money coming into the town; and 1 mark for an extra point such as less waste or fewer flies. A pupil who says only she benefits can earn 1 mark for the position but no more, unless the argument is genuinely developed.*

### Question 7 (9 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Any three: what the clinic actually needs the energy for - light at night, a refrigerator for vaccines, or power for a small machine; how much sunshine, wind or flowing water the village has; what materials can be found nearby and what must be bought; how much money is available; whether anyone in the village can repair the equipment; where the project would be safe from damage and theft.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE sensible things to find out (3 marks). Credit questions about need, about what the site can supply, about materials and money, and about who will repair it. Do NOT credit 'how long it will take to build', which is not what the question asks.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Accept any sensible project. For example, a solar system for the clinic: mount solar panels on the clinic roof facing the sun where no tree shades them; fix a frame, a charge controller, a battery and wiring; connect LED lamps inside and a small refrigerator for vaccines; test each lamp and the refrigerator; label the switches.
- MATERIALS: solar panels, a battery, a charge controller, cable, switches, LED lamps, a frame and fixings - and for a biogas or wind project, the equivalent list.
- STEPS: survey the roof, buy or collect the parts, fit the frame, mount the panels, wire the controller and battery, run the cable inside, fit the lamps, test, and hand over with instructions.
  - *Marking note: Up to 3 marks. 1 mark for a project that would genuinely work for a clinic with no grid supply; 1 mark for a sensible list of materials; and 1 mark for the steps in a sensible order. Cap at 1 if the project is named but the materials and steps are missing. 0 marks for a project that burns charcoal, firewood or diesel, since the question is about sustaining energy locally without harming the environment.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- KNOWING IT WORKS: check that the clinic has light at night and that the refrigerator keeps vaccines cold; read a meter or keep a daily record for a few weeks; compare the clinic's bills or its kerosene use with what it was before.
- KEEPING IT RUNNING: name a person at the clinic and one pupil or teacher to check it; clean the panels; keep a few spare parts and the number of someone who can repair it; fix a card of instructions near the switch; show the next class how it works so the knowledge does not leave with the pupils who built it.
  - *Marking note: Up to 3 marks. 1 mark for how they will know it works - a real check, not just 'we will see'; 1 mark for maintenance - a named person and what they will do; and 1 mark for spares, repairs, written instructions or passing the knowledge on. An answer that says only 'we will test it' earns 0.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Question 3(c) is the most important item on this paper, because it is where pupils show whether they have understood the idea at all. Most will have learned that carbon dioxide is 'bad' and will say the greenhouse effect is harmful, which earns 1 of 3. The full answer is that the effect keeps the earth warm enough to live on and the problem is the EXTRA gas we are adding. Teach both halves every time.
- Question 4(iii) is the blank that catches pupils out. Rotting waste and cattle give out METHANE, not carbon dioxide, and the table gives them the clue in the 'activity' column. The note does not accept carbon dioxide there. Drill the two gases separately: burning fuel and clearing forest give carbon dioxide; rotting waste and cattle give methane.
- Question 1(b) and Question 2(a) both press the same point from different sides - what makes a source renewable, and why charcoal is not one. Pupils will answer 'charcoal is expensive' or 'charcoal makes smoke', which the note does not accept, because the question is about the source running out. Push them to 'trees take years to grow back'.
- Question 6(b) is deliberately open. The strongest answers say the whole community benefits - more food reaches the market, less is wasted, fish can be eaten in the lean season, money comes into the town - while adding that the fishmonger herself gains most directly. A pupil who argues that only she benefits earns 1 for the position but no more unless the argument is genuinely developed.
- Question 7 is the design task from Exemplar 3, and pupils find it easy to NAME a project and hard to list materials and give the steps in order. The note caps them at 1 of 3 if the materials and steps are missing, and gives 0 for anything that burns charcoal, firewood or diesel - which is a common answer and worth warning them about before the examination.
- Question 5(c) asks pupils to CHECK that a change has saved energy, and the mark is on the comparison - a meter reading or a firewood count before and after. 'We will see if it works' earns 0. This is the same skill Question 7(c) needs, so the two reinforce each other.
- Topic weighting is 27 marks each for 19.1, 19.2 and 19.3, and 19 marks for 19.4. The design task carries the least in Section B, where it appears only once. If you spent longer on the project work, move marks towards Question 7.
- Three cheap activities that raise marks sharply: (1) put two thermometers in the sun, one under a clear bowl and one in the open, and read them after ten minutes, which makes the greenhouse effect visible; (2) leave two equal slices of fish or cassava out, one covered and one open to the flies and dust, which teaches Question 6; and (3) have each group cost out one clinic energy project on a single page, which teaches Question 7.
