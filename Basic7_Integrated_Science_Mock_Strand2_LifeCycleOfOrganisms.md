### Question 1 (8 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- A life cycle is the series of changes a living thing goes through during its life.
- It runs from the start of the organism's life (e.g. the egg or the seed) to the time that organism produces young ones of its own.
- The stages then repeat in the next generation.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). Award 1 mark for 'the stages in the life of an organism' if it is not developed further.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- So that the kind of organism does not die out (so the species continues).
- So that the organism replaces those that die.
- So that the organism can increase in number.
- So that the organism can spread to new places.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct reasons (3 marks). Accept 'to produce young ones' once. Do NOT award three marks for the same reason worded three times.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- The last stage (the adult) produces the first stage (the egg) again, so the series comes back to where it started.
- It has no real beginning or end - each generation repeats the same stages.
- A straight line has a beginning and an end, but a life cycle goes round and round.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The key idea is that the adult produces the egg and the sequence repeats.*

### Question 2 (9 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- 1 = egg
- 2 = larva
- 3 = pupa
- 4 = adult
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each (4 marks). The number must be matched to the correct stage. Accept 'maggot' for 2. Do NOT award a mark if the label number is missing.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- The larva (maggot) is a soft, whitish, legless worm-like creature.
- It hatches from the egg.
- It feeds continuously and grows quickly.

  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct features (2 marks). Accept 'it looks like a small white worm'.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). Award at least 1 mark for stating that the change into the adult happens inside the pupa case.*

### Question 3 (6 marks)

**(a)** [2 marks]

- On moist, decaying matter such as refuse, rotten food and animal droppings (manure).
- Reason: when the eggs hatch, the larvae have food to eat immediately.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for the place. 1 mark for the reason (the larvae need food as soon as they hatch). Accept 'in rubbish / in faeces' for the place.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

  - *Marking note: 1 mark for starting with the egg. 1 mark for the correct order of all four stages. Award 0 if any stage is out of order.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Only a few of the eggs are likely to survive to become adults.

  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). The key idea is that most will not survive. Do NOT award a mark for 'so that there will be many flies' on its own.*

### Question 4 (9 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Dead animals
- Rotten food
- Animal droppings (manure)
- Solid waste (refuse / rubbish)
- Liquid waste
- Human food and drink
- Faeces
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct items (3 marks). All of these are named in the curriculum exemplar. Accept 'sugar and other sweet things' as an additional correct answer.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- The housefly has no teeth and cannot chew or bite solid food.
- Its mouthparts are made for sucking up liquid only.
- So it has to turn solid food into a liquid before it can take it in.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). Award at least 1 mark for stating that the housefly can only take in liquid.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- The housefly feeds on filthy matter such as faeces, rotten food and refuse.
- It then lands on the same food that people eat, often within a short time.
- It carries disease organisms on its legs and body hairs, and leaves them behind in its droppings and vomit.
- So the dirty places it feeds on and the clean food people eat are linked by the same fly.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The chain must link the filthy feeding place to human food. Do NOT award a mark for 'the fly is dirty' on its own.*

**(a)** [3 marks]

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Cholera
- Typhoid fever
- Diarrhoea (as a symptom)

**(d)** [3 marks]

  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). Award at least 1 mark for a correct meaning of food poisoning and 1 mark for the role of the fly.*

### Question 6 (12 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Cover all food and drinking water.
- Keep refuse in covered bins and dispose of it properly.
- Clean the house and compound regularly.
- Screen windows and doors with wire mesh.
- Wash hands with soap before eating and after using the toilet.

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Covering food stops the flies from landing on it and contaminating it.
- It also stops them from dropping their droppings and vomit on the food.
- Covering refuse bins stops flies from reaching the decaying matter where they feed and lay eggs.
- So covering breaks the link between the breeding place and human food.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The answer must say something about BOTH food and refuse bins to earn full marks.*

**(c)** [6 marks]

- Choose an intervention, e.g. a clean-up campaign, providing covered refuse bins, digging a refuse pit, or screening food in the market.

- Say when and where it will happen, e.g. every Saturday morning in the school compound and the market.
- Plan how to educate the community - posters, drama, announcements at church or mosque, talking to market women and food sellers.

  - *Marking note: 6 marks. Award 1 mark each for any SIX of the following: (i) a clearly identified problem, (ii) a practical intervention, (iii) who carries it out, (iv) when or where, (v) how the community is educated, and (vi) how the intervention will be checked. Do NOT award marks for an intervention that involves handling insecticides or other chemicals without adult supervision.*

### Question 7 (5 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Draw or collect a picture of each of the four stages: egg, larva (maggot), pupa and adult.
- Put each picture on its own card (or make a small model of each stage from clay, paper or other local material).
- Arrange the cards in the correct order: egg → larva → pupa → adult.
- Use arrows to join the stages, and draw an arrow from the adult back to the egg to close the cycle.
- Label each stage and write one short note about it.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). Award at least 1 mark for producing four separate stages and 1 mark for linking them in the correct order with arrows.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- It makes the order of the stages clear, and shows that the cycle comes back to the egg.
- Pupils can see and handle the stages instead of only hearing about them, so they remember them better.
- It is safer and cleaner than trying to keep live houseflies in the classroom.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct reasons (2 marks). Accept any sensible reason. Do NOT award a mark for 'it is fun' on its own.*
