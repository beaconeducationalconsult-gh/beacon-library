### Question 4 (9 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- A second-class lever is one in which the LOAD lies between the fulcrum and the effort.
- The effort and the load are on the same side of the fulcrum.
- Examples: a wheelbarrow; a nutcracker; a bottle opener; a paper cutter.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The position of the load is essential; award at most 2 marks if only the example is given.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- A third-class lever is one in which the EFFORT lies between the fulcrum and the load.
- The effort and the load are on the same side of the fulcrum, but the effort is the nearer of the two.
- Examples: a pair of sugar tongs; a broom being used to sweep; a fishing rod; the human forearm.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The position of the effort is essential; award at most 2 marks if only the example is given.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Look at the order of the three parts along the lever: fulcrum, load and effort.
- If the FULCRUM is in the middle, between the effort and the load, it is a first-class lever, e.g. a see-saw.
- If the LOAD is in the middle, between the fulcrum and the effort, it is a second-class lever, e.g. a wheelbarrow.
- If the EFFORT is in the middle, between the fulcrum and the load, it is a third-class lever, e.g. a pair of sugar tongs.
- Find the fulcrum first, then see which of the other two lies nearer to it.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). One mark is available for each class correctly described with an example; award at most 2 marks if the examples are missing.*

### Question 6 (9 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Work input is the work done ON a machine, that is, the work put into it.
- Work output is the useful work done BY the machine, that is, the work that comes out of it.
- Both are measured in joules (J).
- Work is calculated as force x distance moved in the direction of the force.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct points (4 marks). Both terms must be defined and the unit given; award at most 3 marks if the unit is missing.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- 50 J of the energy did not come out as useful work.
- It was used to overcome friction in the moving parts of the machine.
- It was changed into heat, and some into sound, which spread out into the surroundings.
- The energy was not destroyed - it changed into forms that are not useful.
- 200 J in minus 150 J out leaves 50 J wasted.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The key ideas are: 50 J is missing, it went to overcome friction, and it became heat and sound. Award at most 2 marks if the pupil says the energy was destroyed.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Some of the work put in is always used to overcome friction in the moving parts.
- Some of it is used to lift the moving parts of the machine itself.
- That energy is changed into heat and sound, which are not useful.
- Because of these losses, the useful work out is always less than the work put in.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). The word to look for is friction.*

### Question 7 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Efficiency is the ratio of work output to work input, given as a percentage.
- It measures how much of the work put into a machine comes out as useful work.
- A more efficient machine wastes less of the work put into it.
- Efficiency = (work output / work input) x 100%.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The formula earns one of the three marks. Accept 'how good a machine is at turning work in into useful work out'.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Efficiency = (work output / work input) x 100%
- Efficiency = (300 / 400) x 100%
- Efficiency = 0.75 x 100%
- Efficiency = 75%
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for the formula, 1 mark for the correct substitution (300 / 400) and 1 mark for the answer 75% (3 marks). Award the substitution and answer marks even if the formula is not written, provided the working is shown. Allow the method mark if the arithmetic is wrong but the substitution is right.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The school should use the machine that is 75 per cent efficient.
- It turns more of the work put into it into useful work.
- It wastes less energy as heat and sound.
- It will cost less to run, because less effort or less fuel is wasted.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for choosing the 75 per cent machine and 1 mark for any ONE correct reason (2 marks). Accept a pupil who chooses the 40 per cent machine only if they give a good reason for it, such as that it is the only one the school can afford or repair.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Question 5(a) is the discriminator. Nearly every pupil gets the claw hammer and the wheelbarrow right; the broom and the nutcracker sort the top from the rest. A broom is third class because the lower hand is the effort, the upper hand is the fulcrum and the load is at the far end - so the effort sits between the other two. Teach that one twice.
- Question 5(c) is the design task and it is marked on a fair test, not on a brilliant idea. The note awards 1 mark for the set-up and 1 mark for what the class would find, and it explicitly looks for the idea that only the length of the handle changes. Do not insist on the crowbar - a spanner on a bolt, or a door pushed near the hinge and then at the handle, works just as well.
- Question 7(b) is the only real calculation, and the note splits it three ways: formula, substitution and answer. Give the method marks generously. A pupil who writes 300 / 400 and then mis-handles the percentage has understood the idea and should not lose everything.
- Question 4(c) is the hardest item on the paper. The reliable route is to find the fulcrum first, then ask which of the other two lies in the middle. Pupils who try to memorise three separate definitions without that rule usually mix up the second and third classes.
- Topic weighting is 32 marks for 14.9, 36 for 14.10 and 32 for 14.11. Levers carry the most because the curriculum asks for naming, classifying AND demonstrating all three classes. If you spent longer on machines than on efficiency, move marks towards Questions 1 and 2.
- Two cheap practicals that raise marks sharply: (1) push a door open first near the hinge and then at the handle, and (2) lift a stone with a short crowbar and then with a long one. The first teaches Questions 3 to 5, the second teaches Question 5(c).
- The companion paper covers topics 14.1 to 14.8 - force, balanced and unbalanced forces, inertia, Newton's First Law and its applications, and the types, behaviour and uses of magnets - under content standard B7/JHS1.4.4.1. Use the two papers together for a full end-of-term examination on this sub-strand, or separately as two class tests.
