## 7. PUBLICATION ANSWER KEY SPECIFICATION

### 7.1 Answer Key Book Structure

Each answer key book should contain:

#### Front Matter
1. **Title page** — "[Book Title] — Answer Key & Marking Scheme"
2. **Copyright page** — Publisher information, edition, ISBN
3. **How to use this answer key** — Instructions for teachers
4. **Table of contents** — List of papers with page numbers
5. **General marking principles** — Standard rules applicable to all papers

#### Main Content (per paper)
1. **Paper header** — Paper number, topic, strand, content standard
2. **Section A answer key** — Table with Q#, Answer, Curriculum Area, Cognitive Level
3. **Answer distribution summary** — A/B/C/D counts
4. **Cognitive level distribution** — Bloom's taxonomy breakdown
5. **Section B marking scheme** — Full marking scheme with general rules, per-question marks, model answers, marking notes
6. **Curriculum coverage summary** — Content standards and indicators covered

#### Back Matter
1. **Curriculum coverage matrix** — Full matrix showing all content standards covered across all papers
2. **Cognitive demand summary** — Aggregate Bloom's taxonomy distribution across all papers
3. **Difficulty distribution summary** — Easy/moderate/challenging breakdown
4. **Author queries** — Any unresolved issues flagged during production

### 7.2 General Marking Principles (Standard for All Books)

```
GENERAL MARKING RULES

1. Accept any other correct and relevant answer not listed in this scheme.
2. In calculations, award method marks for a correct formula and correct 
   substitution even when the final arithmetic is wrong.
3. A correct numerical answer without the required unit loses the final 
   mark for that part only.
4. Where a candidate gives more answers than asked for, mark only the 
   first ones up to the number required.
5. Do not deduct marks for spelling unless the meaning is changed.
6. In a drawing, marks are for correct shape and correct labels, not for 
   beauty; every label line must touch the part it names.
7. Accept the singular or plural form of scientific terms (e.g., 
   mitochondrion or mitochondria).
8. Where a candidate names a material or example from their own community 
   that the scheme does not list, award the mark if the science/maths is correct.
```
