### Question 2 (9 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Balanced forces are forces acting on an object that are equal in size and opposite in direction.
- Balanced forces cancel each other out, so they give no overall (resultant) force.
- Unbalanced forces are forces acting on an object that are NOT equal and opposite.
- Unbalanced forces do not cancel out; there is an overall force in one direction.
- Only an unbalanced force can start an object moving, stop it, speed it up, slow it down or change its direction.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct points (4 marks). At least ONE point must describe BALANCED forces and at least one UNBALANCED forces; award at most 3 marks if only one kind is described.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- It does not start moving; if it was at rest, it stays at rest.
- If it was already moving, it keeps moving at the same speed and in the same direction.
- Its motion does not change at all.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). The key idea is that the motion does not change.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- The box moves towards the boy pushing with the smaller force, that is, towards the boy pushing with 45 N.
- The forces are not balanced, because 60 N is bigger than 45 N.
- There is an overall (resultant) force of 15 N acting towards the 45 N side.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for the correct direction, 1 mark for saying the forces are unbalanced and 1 mark for the reason (60 N is bigger than 45 N) (3 marks). Accept 'towards the weaker boy'. The overall force of 15 N earns the reason mark even if not calculated.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- A bar magnet

**(c)** [4 marks]

- This works because a magnet attracts strongly at its poles while plain steel does not attract at all.

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Attraction is when two magnets pull towards each other.
- Attraction happens when unlike poles are brought together, that is a north pole and a south pole.
- Repulsion is when two magnets push each other away.
- Repulsion happens when like poles are brought together, that is north and north, or south and south.
- Repulsion is the surest test that both bars are magnets, because a magnet will attract plain iron but only a magnet will repel.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). At least one point must describe attraction and at least one repulsion; award at most 2 marks if only one is described.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- The magnet turns and finally comes to rest pointing in the north-south direction.
- The same end always points to the north; that end is called the north pole (the north-seeking pole).
- This behaviour is used in a magnetic compass to find direction.
- It is useful to travellers, pilots, sailors and anyone who needs to find the way.
- The magnet lines up because the earth itself behaves like a very large magnet.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The pupil must describe what happens AND say how it is used to earn full marks.*

**(c)** [4 marks]

- A magnetic compass for finding direction.
- A catch that keeps a refrigerator door or a cupboard door shut.
- Holding notes, charts or photographs on a magnetic board at school or in the office.
- Picking up small steel pins, nails or screws that have fallen.
- Separating iron and steel from a mixture with other materials.
- In a door bell, a loudspeaker or an electric motor.
- In a magnetic wallet clasp or a bag fastener.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct uses (4 marks). Accept any sensible everyday use of a magnet.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Question 3(c) is the item to watch. Pupils routinely say the vehicle 'threw' them forward, as if something pushed them. It did not: the passenger was already moving forward at the same speed as the vehicle, and when the vehicle braked nothing braked the passenger, so his own inertia carried him on. Allow the two reason marks only for answers that make this point.
- Question 6(c) is the design task, and it is deliberately open. Three different tests are listed in the marking scheme - suspending the bar, dipping it into iron pins, and touching one end to the middle of the other bar - and all three earn full marks. Do not insist on the one you taught.
- Question 4(a) is a four-mark recall of Newton's First Law, and the note withholds a mark if the word UNBALANCED is missing. Balanced forces are already acting on every object at rest; it takes an unbalanced force to change the motion. Teach that word before the mock.
- The magnet and the mechanics halves of this paper are both under the same content standard, B7/JHS1.4.4.1, which is titled 'motion, Newton's first law of motion, magnetic force in relation to motion'. That is why magnets sit in the same paper as forces - it is the curriculum's own grouping, not mine.
- Topic weighting is 13 marks each for topics 14.1 to 14.6 and 11 each for 14.7 and 14.8. If you spent longer on magnets than on forces, move marks towards Questions 6 and 7.
- Two cheap practicals that raise marks sharply: (1) flick a card from under a coin on a glass, and (2) float a magnetised needle on a leaf in a bowl of water and watch it settle north-south. The first teaches Questions 3 and 5, the second teaches Question 7(b).
- The companion paper covers topics 14.9 to 14.11 - simple machines, the three classes of lever, and work input, work output and efficiency - under content standard B7/JHS1.4.4.2. Use the two papers together for a full end-of-term examination on this sub-strand, or separately as two class tests.
