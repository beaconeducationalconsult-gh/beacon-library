### Question 1 (10 marks)

**(a)** [5 marks]

- Carbohydrates
- Proteins
- Fats and oils (fatty acids)

- Water
- Roughage or fibre
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FIVE correct classes (5 marks). Accept any five of the seven. Do NOT accept a named food such as 'rice' or 'beans' - the question asks for the CLASS of nutrient.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Carbohydrate - provides energy for work, play and warmth.
- Protein - builds the body and repairs worn-out tissues.
- Fat and oil - provides energy, keeps the body warm and helps the body absorb some vitamins.
- Vitamin - protects the body from disease and keeps parts such as the eyes and gums healthy.
- Mineral salt - builds hard parts such as bones and teeth, and helps body processes such as making blood.
- Water - carries substances round the body and helps to control body temperature.
- Roughage or fibre - helps the food to move through the gut and prevents constipation.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for each class correctly matched to a correct use (3 marks). The class must be one of those named in (a) and the use must belong to THAT class; award no mark for a correct use attached to the wrong class.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- A child's body is growing, so new tissue is being made all the time.
- Protein is the nutrient the body uses to build new tissue and to repair worn-out tissue.
- Without enough protein the child stops growing properly and becomes weak.
- A child that lacks protein over a long time may develop kwashiorkor.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). The key idea is that protein BUILDS tissue and a growing child is making new tissue. Do NOT accept 'protein gives energy' on its own.*

### Question 3 (9 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- A deficiency disease is an illness caused by a lack of an essential nutrient in the diet.
- It is not caught from another person and it is not caused by germs.
- It happens when the body does not get enough of a nutrient over a long period.
- It can be prevented or cured by eating the missing nutrient.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The key word is LACK of a nutrient. Do NOT accept 'a disease caused by germs'.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Not eating enough food in general.
- Not eating enough of one particular nutrient, such as protein or iron.
- Eating the same few foods all the time instead of a varied diet.
- Poverty, so that the family cannot afford foods with the needed nutrients.
- Poor feeding habits, such as a child being given only one kind of food.
- Ignorance of what makes up a balanced diet.
- Illness that stops the body absorbing the nutrient even when it is eaten.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct causes (3 marks). Accept any sensible cause.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- No single food contains all the classes of nutrient the body needs.
- So a person who eats only one kind of food gets plenty of one nutrient but misses the others.
- The missing nutrient is the one that causes the deficiency disease.
- Eating a variety of foods makes sure that all the nutrients are present.
- A child fed only on maize, for example, may get energy but miss protein and vitamins.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The key idea is that one food cannot supply every nutrient.*

### Question 4 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- SYMPTOMS are the signs that can be seen or felt in the patient, and that the doctor or nurse looks for.
- EFFECTS are what the disease does to the body and to the person's life.
- A symptom is what you notice, such as pale eyes or bleeding gums.
- An effect is the harm that follows, such as a child failing to grow or being too weak to walk to school.
- For anaemia: the symptom is paleness and tiredness; the effect is that the child cannot concentrate or play.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct points (4 marks). At least ONE mark must come from explaining symptoms and at least ONE from explaining effects; award at most 3 marks if only one of the two is explained.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Teaching families to eat a balanced diet is better.
- A balanced diet gives all the nutrients, not only the ones in the tablet.
- The habit lasts for life, while the tablets stop when the supply stops.
- Local foods are cheaper and always available; tablets have to be bought and delivered.
- Tablets can help in an emergency, but they do not teach the family anything.
- Accept a pupil who argues for tablets only if they give a good reason, such as that the tablets work faster for a child who is already ill.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for a clear position and 1 mark each for any THREE good reasons (4 marks). Credit either side of the argument, provided the reasons genuinely support the position taken. An answer with no reason scores at most 1.*

### Question 5 (9 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Night blindness - lack of vitamin A.
- Rickets - lack of vitamin D, together with calcium and phosphorus.
- Scurvy - lack of vitamin C.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for each disease correctly matched to its missing nutrient (3 marks). The nutrient must be matched to the disease. For rickets accept vitamin D alone or vitamin D with calcium.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Night blindness - the child cannot see clearly in dim light or after dusk, and bumps into things.
- Rickets - the bones are soft and bent, giving bow legs, a bent spine or a knock-kneed look.
- Scurvy - the gums swell and bleed, teeth may loosen, and wounds heal slowly.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for each correct symptom (3 marks). The symptom must be matched to the disease.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Eat foods containing vitamin D, such as fish, egg yolk, milk and fortified spreads.
- Eat foods containing calcium and phosphorus, such as milk, fish with soft bones, groundnuts and green leafy vegetables.
- Let the skin get some gentle morning sunlight, which helps the body make its own vitamin D.
- Give babies and young children the foods above from an early age, so the bones harden properly.
- Take a child with soft or bent bones to the clinic or hospital for treatment.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). At least ONE point must mention vitamin D and at least ONE must mention calcium or sunlight; award at most 2 marks if only one of these is given.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- Kwashiorkor is caused by a lack of PROTEIN.
- The belly swells with fluid.
- The arms and legs become very thin.
- The hair becomes thin, dry and reddish or light in colour.
- The child stops growing, is miserable and loses interest in food.
- The skin may crack and peel, and sores heal slowly.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for naming protein and 1 mark each for any TWO correct symptoms (3 marks). Award at most 2 marks if the nutrient is wrong but the symptoms are right.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- Iron is needed to make haemoglobin, the red substance in the blood that carries oxygen.
- Without enough iron the body cannot make enough healthy red blood cells.
- So the blood carries less oxygen than the body needs.
- Without oxygen the cells cannot release energy from food, so the child has little energy.
- That is why the child tires quickly, feels weak and looks pale.
- The child is also likely to fall ill more often, because the body is run down.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The chain to look for is: lack of iron means less haemoglobin, less haemoglobin means less oxygen carried, and less oxygen means less energy. Award at most 1 mark for an answer that only says 'the blood is not enough'.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- Liver
- Beans and other legumes
- Dark green leafy vegetables such as kontomire or alefu
- Fish
- Meat
- Eggs
- Groundnuts
- Fortified cereals or flour
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct foods (2 marks). Accept any food genuinely rich in iron. Do NOT accept milk - it is a good food but it is poor in iron.*

### Question 7 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- The children with swollen bellies, thin arms and legs and reddish hair have KWASHIORKOR.
- The nutrient they lack is PROTEIN.
- The children who cannot find their way in the evening have NIGHT BLINDNESS.
- The nutrient they lack is VITAMIN A.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for each disease correctly named and 1 mark for each nutrient correctly matched (4 marks). The disease and the nutrient must go together. Accept the pair in either order.*

**(b)** [4 marks]

- Tell the families to add protein foods to the children's meals - beans, groundnuts, fish, eggs or soya.
- Tell them to add foods rich in vitamin A - red palm oil, eggs, liver, dark green leafy vegetables, mango and pawpaw.
- Explain that the children will then get the nutrients they are missing and will grow properly again.
- Show them how to mix the foods they already grow, rather than asking them to buy new ones.
- Advise them to take the worst-affected children to the clinic or health centre.
- Suggest a small demonstration meal or a talk at the next community meeting so the whole village hears the same message.
- Explain that the changes must be kept up, not done once, or the children will fall ill again.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR correct points (4 marks). This is a design task: award method marks for any sensible answer. At least ONE point must be about protein and at least ONE about vitamin A; award at most 3 marks if only one of the two is covered. Saying WHAT to do and WHY it helps are both creditworthy.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Question 4(a) is the one most pupils muddle. They use 'symptoms' and 'effects' as if they were the same word. They are not: the symptom is what you can see or what the patient complains of, such as bleeding gums; the effect is the harm that follows, such as a child being too weak to walk to school. The note pays one mark for each half and caps the answer at 3 if only one is explained.
- Question 6(b) is the discriminator. Nearly every pupil can say 'the blood is not enough', but the marks are on the chain behind it: too little iron means too little haemoglobin, too little haemoglobin means less oxygen carried, and less oxygen means less energy released. Teach that chain as three linked sentences and the marks follow.
- Question 2(a) is the paper's factual core, and the two blanks that catch pupils out are (iii) and (v). Protein is for GROWTH and REPAIR, not for energy - pupils give 'energy' for almost every nutrient. Fat gives energy and warmth and helps absorb some vitamins.
- Question 1(b) is marked on the match, not on the list. A pupil who writes three correct uses but attaches them to the wrong classes scores nothing. Tell them to write the class and the use together in one sentence.
- Question 7 is the task that carries the indicator's real demand: the curriculum asks pupils to RELATE the nutrients they lack to the foods they normally eat. The note requires advice on protein and on vitamin A, and it rewards answers that use foods the village already grows rather than foods it would have to buy.
- Topic weighting is 30 marks for 17.1, 32 for 17.2 and 38 for 17.3. The named diseases carry the most, because Exemplar 4 asks for the symptoms, effects and prevention of several of them. If you spent longer on nutrients than on the diseases, move marks towards Questions 1 and 2.
- Two cheap practicals that raise marks sharply: (1) bring in a week of real food labels or empty wrappers and let pupils sort them by the nutrient they mainly supply, and (2) look at a sample of dark green leafy vegetables, red palm oil and beans on a table and ask which disease each would prevent. The first teaches Questions 1 and 2, the second teaches Questions 5 and 7.
- The companion paper covers topics 17.4 to 17.7 - the nature of viral diseases, COVID-19, Ebola and H1N1, and the roles of individuals, communities and government in managing them - under content standard B7/JHS1.5.2.2. Use the two papers together for a full end-of-term examination on this sub-strand, or separately as two class tests.
