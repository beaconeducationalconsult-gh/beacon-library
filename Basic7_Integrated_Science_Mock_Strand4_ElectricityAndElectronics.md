### Question 4 (9 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Items needed: two 1.5 V dry cells, a cell holder, a switch, a resistor, an LED and connecting wires.
- Put the two dry cells in the holder in series - the positive of one touching the negative of the other - to make a 3 V battery.
- Join the positive end of the battery to one leg of the switch with a wire.
- Join the other leg of the switch to one end of the resistor, then join the other end of the resistor to the LONGER (positive) leg of the LED.
- Join the shorter (negative) leg of the LED back to the negative end of the battery to complete the loop.
- Close the switch to test the circuit - the LED should light.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for naming the items, 1 mark for connecting the cells in series, 1 mark for joining the components in one loop that includes the resistor and the LED, and 1 mark for a correct final check such as closing the switch to see whether the LED lights (4 marks). **(b)** [3 marks]

- When the switch is CLOSED the circuit is complete, so current flows round the circuit and the LED lights.

  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The pupil must deal with BOTH closing and opening to earn full marks; award at most 2 marks if only one of the two is described.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The resistor limits the current so that too much does not flow through the LED.

### Question 5 (8 marks)

**(a)** [3 marks]

- The P region is the positive region, and it is called the anode.
- The N region is the negative region, and it is called the cathode.
- The two regions are joined together inside the diode at the junction, and this is what makes the diode allow current to flow in one direction only.
- P stands for Positive and N stands for Negative.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). At least one point must describe the P region and at least one the N region; award at most 2 marks if only one is described.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- The negative end is marked with a band or a ring painted round the body of the diode.
- The leg at the negative end is the shorter of the two legs.
- The flat side of some diode packages marks the negative end.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct ways (2 marks). The band or ring is the answer to look for first.*

**(c)** [3 marks]

- Connected the right way round (P side to the positive of the battery), the diode allows current to flow, so the LED lights.
- Connected the wrong way round, the diode blocks the current, so the LED does not light.
- Nothing is damaged - the LED simply stays off until the diode is turned round.
- This shows that a diode conducts in one direction only, which is its main use in a circuit.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The pupil must describe BOTH the right way round and the wrong way round to earn full marks; award at most 2 marks if only one is described. Do NOT accept 'the diode breaks' or 'the battery is spoilt'.*

### Question 6 (8 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- 1 - switch: it opens and closes the circuit, so it switches the current on and off.
- 2 - resistor: it limits or controls the amount of current flowing in the circuit.
- 3 - battery (two dry cells in series): it provides the voltage that pushes the current round the circuit.
- 4 - LED: it changes electrical energy into light, and shows that current is flowing.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for all FOUR parts correctly named AND their functions stated (4 marks). A correctly named part with no function earns only half a mark each. Accept 'two dry cells' for 3. Accept 'lamp that gives light' for 4, but 'LED' is the expected answer.*

**(b)** [2 marks]

- Each component has a job to do and the circuit only works if every one of them does its job.
- The battery alone cannot light the LED - it needs wires and a closed switch to carry the current.
- The resistor controls how much current reaches the LED, so it decides how bright the LED is and whether the LED is damaged.
- If any one part is missing, faulty or disconnected, the loop is broken and the whole circuit stops working.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any TWO correct points (2 marks). The key idea is that the parts form one loop and each one affects what the others can do.*

**(c)** [2 marks]

- The LED becomes dimmer.
- Adding another resistor increases the total resistance in the circuit.
- More resistance means less current flows, and less current makes the LED dimmer.
- If enough resistors were added, the LED would eventually go out.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark for saying that the LED becomes dimmer and 1 mark for the reason that the extra resistance reduces the current (2 marks). Accept 'it gets less bright'. Do NOT accept 'it becomes brighter'.*

### Question 7 (7 marks)

**(a)** [4 marks]

- Resistors - they limit or control the current in the different parts of the radio.
- Capacitors - they store electric charge and release it when it is needed.
- Diodes - they allow current to flow in one direction only.
- LEDs - they show that the radio is switched on, or they light up the display.
- Inductors (coils of wire) - they store energy in a magnetic field and help to tune the radio to a station.
- A switch or a volume control - it opens, closes or varies the circuit.
- Connecting wires or a circuit board - they join all the other parts together.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any FOUR components correctly named with what each one does (4 marks). A correctly named component with no function earns only half a mark each. Accept any component that would genuinely be found inside a radio.*

**(b)** [3 marks]

- The parts form one circuit, so each one depends on the others to do its job.
- The battery or power supply alone cannot make the radio work - the current must reach every part through the wires and the circuit board.
- The resistors and capacitors set the conditions in which the diodes and the transistors can work.
- If one part is missing, faulty or disconnected, the radio stops working even though all the other parts are still in place.
- This is what is meant by interdependence: the gadget works only when the parts work together.
  - *Marking note: 1 mark each for any THREE correct points (3 marks). The key idea is that the gadget works only when all the parts work together, and that one faulty part stops the whole thing.*

### EXAMINER NOTES

- Fig. 1 is drawn with text characters because this is a plain Markdown file. Before printing, redraw it with the standard circuit symbols so that the dry cell has one long plate and one short plate and so that the LED carries the two arrows that show it gives out light. The numbering (1-4) is what the questions depend on. Key: 1 = switch, 2 = resistor, 3 = battery (two dry cells in series), 4 = LED.
- Question 4(a) is the design task, and it comes straight from exemplar 2 of B7/JHS1.4.2.2.2 - 'construct a simple electronic circuit comprising a 3V battery made of two dry cells in series with a switch and an LED'. Award method marks: any workable plan that is clearly described deserves credit. The two things to look for are the cells joined positive-to-negative in series, and the longer leg of the LED on the positive side.
- Question 6(a) asks for the name AND the function of each labelled part, so a pupil who only names four parts earns half marks. Say this to the class before the paper if you use it for practice.
- Question 2(c) is a debate question with no single right answer - that is deliberate, and it follows exemplar 1 of B7/JHS1.4.2.1.2. Both choices are valid. Award the three reasons only if they actually support the choice the pupil made, and accept a pupil who rejects both and argues for solar or hydro instead.
- B7 pupils are NOT expected to remember the resistor colour-code chart. Question 3(c) only asks what the bands are FOR, and the marking note says so explicitly.
- Keep the inductor light. It is named in exemplar 1 of B7/JHS1.4.2.2.3, but 'stores energy in a magnetic field and opposes changes in the current' is as far as B7 goes; do not ask for coil calculations or reactance.
- Topic weighting is even at 16 to 17 marks for each of topics 12.1 to 12.5, with 19 for 12.6, because 12.6 carries three of the five indicators. If your scheme of work spent longer on generation than on components, move marks towards Questions 1 and 2.
- If your school has a science kit, let the class actually build the circuit in Question 4 before the mock. Pupils who have handled a resistor and an LED write far better answers, and it is the activity the indicator asks for.
